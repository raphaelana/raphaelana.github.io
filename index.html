Bio: I am a PhD student in Computer Science at UMass Lowell, advised by Prof. Mohammad Arif Ul Alam, supported by an NSF grant. My research focuses on graph neural networks, spatial machine learning, and health-focused AI — building systems that transform fragmented, privacy-sensitive data into actionable risk maps for cities and clinicians.

My work connects two themes: graph-based relational modeling under real-world constraints (incomplete data, privacy regulations, resource-limited deployment), and non-invasive AI diagnostics from everyday audio signals.

Research Focus

  • Environmental Health AI: Developing spatial Graph Attention Network (GAT) frameworks to predict neighborhood-scale lead contamination in drinking water using publicly available housing, demographic, infrastructure, and geographic data — bypassing costly physical sampling to produce block-level risk scores aligned with EPA regulations.
  • Clinical Audio & Speech Processing: Applying Graph Neural Networks (GCN/GAT) to non-invasive disease detection — modeling conversational structure for dementia screening (AST+GAT) and classifying lung sounds across six respiratory conditions for edge-device deployment (CNN-GCN).

Current Directions & Impact

  • Lead Contamination Risk Mapping: Validated across Chicago (38,000+ water tests), Flint, MI (14,000+ homes), and Massachusetts municipalities. Presented to the U.S. EPA and the Merrimack River Watershed Council. Featured at AAAI 2026.
  • Dementia Screening: Capturing cognitive decline through inter-segment discourse relationships in spontaneous speech — validated on DementiaBank Pitt Corpus and TAUKADIAL. Presented at IEEE ICASSP 2026.
  • Respiratory Diagnostics: Graph-based lung sound classification across six clinical conditions on 1,000+ recordings, designed for resource-limited clinical deployment. Presented at ACM/IEEE CHASE 2025.
