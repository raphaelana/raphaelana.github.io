I build graph neural networks for public-health problems where labeled data is scarce and records are incomplete.

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at UMass Lowell, advised by Prof. Mohammad Arif Ul Alam in the CUBICS Lab and supported by NSF Award #2230180. My work predicts lead contamination in drinking water from public housing, Census, and EPA data, and detects dementia and respiratory disease from everyday audio.

The lead-risk work spans Chicago, Flint, and Massachusetts, and I have presented it to the U.S. EPA and the Merrimack River Watershed Council. Before my PhD I worked as a software engineer building backend services and REST APIs.

Research
  • Environmental health AISpatial graph attention networks that estimate lead-contamination risk from public housing, demographic, and infrastructure data, so cities can target testing where physical sampling is limited.
  • Clinical audioGraph-based models over speech and lung sounds for non-invasive screening: dementia detection from conversational speech (AST + GAT) and respiratory disease classification (GCN).
  • Current work: learning with few labelsSelf-supervised pretraining, graph-conditioned diffusion models that generate synthetic training records, and transfer across cities.

Self-Supervised Graph Attention Networks for Community-Engaged Lead Contamination Risk Assessment
Raphael Anaadumba, Nazim A. Belabbaci, Yigit Bozkurt, Connor Sullivan, et al.
Scientific Reports, 2026Environmental health
Self-supervised pretraining lets a GAT learn lead risk from public housing and historical testing data: 90% accuracy, 0.836 AUC, up to 12% above prior state of the art, with a five-fold reduction in screening cost.
